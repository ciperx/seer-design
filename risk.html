06 — risk pricing · soundings

Soundings — depth, measured
before you cross

A single market gives you one number: the price of a yes. Soundings treats risk as terrain instead. It samples market-implied default probability across ten protocols, fits each to a curve of executable quotes at seven size bands, and returns a loss-given-default figure you can put beside another protocol's — at the size you would actually take, not at the size the headline quote assumes.

expected loss = PD × LGD. Aave v3 → 3.10% × 4,200 bps = 130 bps. Across the board the range runs 81 bps (Lido) to 373 bps (Maple): identical headlines, very different severity once a default lands. LGD is modelled, not observed — see the methodology note below.

One price, or a surface

The difference is not precision. It is whether size changes the answer.

Single market

binary

One quote, one size, no shape. Ask what happens at 10× and the market has nothing to say — the number does not move because there is no second observation to move it to.

The grid

surveyed

Seven size bands, three probability contours. The vertical gap between contours is skew — the price of being wrong at size, which a single yes/no market quietly refuses to quote.

The deepest book on the board, and the one where the curves disagree least.

read: at 10M the book quotes just above expected loss — 137 against 130 — which is what a liquid market looks like when it broadly agrees with the model. By 50M the same book wants 246 bps. That 109 bps gap is the honest cost of size, and it is invisible on a single-price venue.

methodology. Curves are linear fits over executable quotes sampled at seven size bands (0.5, 2, 5, 10, 20, 35, 50 $M), reweighted by realised fill ratio so that quotes nobody could actually hit contribute less. Contours mark premium at fixed default-probability bands. LGD is backed out as expected loss ÷ PD — 130 ÷ 3.1% = 4,194, rounded to 4,200 — then cross-checked against the median implied LGD across bands. Confidence tags on the board above track quote count, venue count and 7-day drift, not credit quality. Survey window 20 Oct 2026, 14:00 UTC. Model v0.4.

What this is not

  • Not insurance. Nothing on this page transfers risk. There is no counterparty to you, no policy, no claim, no payout — only a reading of what other people's quotes imply.
  • Not a promise of safety. A depth score of 92 says the book is deep, not that the protocol is sound. Deep books have been wrong before, together, on the same day.
  • Not advice, and not a forecast. Market-implied probability is a price, and prices can be wrong for a quarter at a time. The model is fitted to quotes that were live at 14:00 UTC; it knows nothing after that.
