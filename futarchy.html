Decision memo

What the two prices say

If PIP-27 passes and the KPI is met, the market implies +$2.1M in annualised protocol revenue. Under the fail condition it implies −$0.4M. The pass branch trades at 64.1¢, the fail branch at 22.9¢, and the 41.2-point gap is the market's stated confidence that the fee increase does what the proposal claims.

That gap is not enthusiasm. A 64.1¢ contract on a $2.1M gain against a $0.4M loss is an argument about probability, not about size — the market thinks the fee probably works, and that it is not worth much if it does.

Mechanical recommendation: vote yes. Break-even sits at a 16.0% pass probability; the book prices 64.1%. The edge is real and thin, and it rests on a KPI nobody can observe until February.

What the market is not saying

  • It is not a vote. Prices are a joint forecast of outcome, not a preference poll. The governor tally decides, and it does not read the book.
  • The books are thin. $509K across both sides is informative, not resistant. A $40K order moves the pass market roughly four points.
  • Right about the fee, wrong about the KPI. Revenue can rise while volume quietly shrinks. The metric cannot see retention, churn or the accounts that leave.
  • Resolution is human. If Treasury accounting changes mid-window, the resolver decides what counts — and that decision is not tradeable.
  • Positions are correlated. 61% of pass-market liquidity sits with four addresses that also hold governance tokens. Their forecasts are not independent.
  • A price is not a promise. 64.1¢ means the market would take the other side at 35.9¢. It does not mean the fee will work.
