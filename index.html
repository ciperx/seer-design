One protocol.
Six ways to price what happens next.

Seer is the market layer beneath a family of applications. Each application asks a different question — will this proposal work, who will be selected, how risky is this exposure — and each answer arrives as a price you can compare instead of an opinion you have to trust.

The six connected interfaces

Same protocol. Different question.

Every interface shares one market engine, one resolution record and one identity layer. What changes is the shape of the question and the person asking it.

01 · explorerlive

Seer app

Every market in one place. Search, filter, read the book, take a position. The index of the protocol rather than a bet on any single theme.
02 · deeplive

Deep

Questions with many possible outcomes — including markets built for agents to trade. A field of grant proposals becomes a distribution, not a coin flip.
03 · futarchylive

Futarchy

Conditional markets on a proposal: what the world looks like if it passes versus if it fails. The decision stays with the DAO; the prices inform it.
04 · opportunitylive

Opportunity markets

Which submission is likely to be selected? Traders forecast the pick, reviewers keep the decision, and the spread shows where the crowd disagrees.
05 · risk pricinglive

Soundings

Market estimates of risk across protocols, compared side by side instead of read as one yes-or-no price in isolation.
06 · settlementcore

Resolution record

The shared truth layer: sources, dispute windows and payout history behind every market on every surface. Nothing above it can drift from it.
How it fits together

The applications are views. The protocol is the ledger.

Each surface writes to the same market primitive: a question, a resolution source, a settlement date and a book. That is why a trader who learns one interface already knows the others, and why a price on Deep can be read next to a price on Futarchy without translation.

Design principle

A price is a measurement, not a promise. Nothing on these interfaces tells you what is safe — they tell you what the market currently believes, how deep that belief is, and how much it has moved. The decision stays with the reader.
